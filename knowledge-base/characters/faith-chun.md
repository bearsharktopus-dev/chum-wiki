## Background

Faith Chun leads the Boston Red Shocks as the Colonel. The cell's members describe their work as "aggressively investigative journalism" with occasional property damage, and spend their days in a Northeast mutual-aid network. She recruited Claymore after finding her in an abandoned shipping facility in South Boston ("we could use someone with your skill set"), and runs training out of a condemned factory in Everett.

When the Red Shocks move their campaign to Philadelphia, she uses the name Faith Song for the civilian side of the work, running a Boston mutual-aid crew's food and supply distribution in the neighborhood damaged by the South Street disaster: "Philadelphia's where it's bad... Somebody's got to do the boring part."

## Powers & Abilities

### PERKS Assessment

Unknown.

### Abilities

- **Proportionate armament** - In her own words, she reaches into her satchel and draws "a weapon proportionate to whatever's threatening me." The weapons escalate with the threat, and "I have never found the bottom." Against a warehouse crew she produces a pistol and a foam grenade; against a forty-foot Tyrannosaurus she produces a boat flare gun, then a .50 BMG anti-materiel rifle.
- **Command** - Builds operational plans quickly (nine minutes for the Grays Ferry raid) and issues short, precise orders.
- **Logistics** - Runs a distribution lot "like they've done it a hundred times, because they have, just not here," down to a whiteboard column counting strollers on porches.
- **Situational awareness** - Checks the street both ways before choosing a direction, and looks at Sam "the way you look at something you already knew was going to be there."
- **Firearms** - Carries a Walther PPK, spraypainted hazard yellow on the trigger and grip, for when her power is not enough; Sam notices a holster under her jacket.

### Limitations

- Not physically durable; a tail strike puts her in a dumpster with likely broken ribs and a leg injury.

## Personality

Economical in everything - "No wasted motion, no unnecessary words." Claymore learned more from a week of her silences than from hours of Ghostwriter's monologues, and Sam sees her as having two visible states, "at-rest and processing." Her one visible pressure valve is a rehearsed joke: asked where she is from, she answers "North Korea," perfectly flat, then adds that "the joke works better on federal employees" and that "airport employees really hate when you do the North Korea bit." She has a dry wit ("Taller. Everyone does. I'm four ten without the boots, too") and considers herself ideological rather than mercenary: "Unlike you, I'm not fighting for money or power. I'm fighting for an ideology." She will not name the Kingdom's "Bullfrog" program publicly, reasoning that "we say its name and it stops being findable."

??? warning "Book 19: Front Towards Enemy"

    **Role:** Supporting

    As Faith Song, she visits the Tacony Community Center with a flyer for her crew's Thursday supply run, tells the North Korea joke, and clocks Sam immediately; Sam spots her holster. When Sam mentions "Boston people... Faith something - Song, maybe?" to Abigail, the reporter shows no recognition. Sam switches shifts and brings Alex Kirby to the South Street run, where the crew works with practiced efficiency. At the end of the run Faith thanks Sam for bringing "a strong kid," mentions that the crew is adding a Sunday, and says: "Community absorbs a lot, if you build it right. Even in a bad season. We're not gonna be here forever, but, well. You know, you Philadelphians, and all that." Sam skips the Sunday run over the holiday and her own distrust (Chapter 287).

    In Chapter 291, in costume as the Colonel, she stands with a megaphone on a Center City parapet beside a fireworks display, denouncing Philadelphia's government, criminals, and "a literal monster," and ending "WATCH US WORK. GIVE US TARGETS. OR GET OUT OF THE WAY." No one is hurt, and no group claims it.

    In Interlude CM.2, a tip-line call leads the Red Shocks to a Kingdom warehouse in Grays Ferry. The Colonel walks in through the front in dress uniform - braid, epaulettes, red sash, domino mask - and is recognized as the rooftop lady. The team finds glowing powder, clay amphorae labeled for return to "Z," and a "BULLFROG" binder before Kingdom reinforcements arrive: Mr. Tyrannosaur, who calls her speech cool and transforms into a forty-foot T. rex, and a woman with knives. Her flare gun buys time; thrown into a dumpster, she draws an anti-materiel rifle and forces Mr. T to stand down. He warns that the Red Shocks are now "on a list."

    Injured and swearing in Korean, she finds a tracker on the van, orders a new safehouse, keeps "Bullfrog" out of the public statement, and has the powder, the Kingdom, and Rex Rampage written up for "the journo." The statement runs the next day (Chapter 292).

## Trivia

- She says she is "four ten without the boots"; Claymore's narration describes her as "a 4'11" Korean woman in 1800s military gear," and Sam estimates "four-ten, maybe, in boots."
- As Faith Song she wears her black hair pinned back and a large canvas messenger bag cross-body.
- Her verdict on the Grays Ferry raid, delivered to an injured team: "Well. Mission success." Nobody laughs.
