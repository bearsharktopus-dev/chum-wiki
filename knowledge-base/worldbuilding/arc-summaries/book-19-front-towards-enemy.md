## Summary

Book 19 opens on Sam's first day of senior year, about two months after Fatima's departure and the South Street firebombing. The city is under curfew, a Gallup poll puts faith in local superheroes at a twenty-year low, and Sam is trying to be a "grind girl": AP Chemistry and Calculus, one Thursday EMT shift, one weekly session with Lina, mentorship, and a visible Megalodon patrol loop through Mayfair. On her first shift she treats a man in a Center City alley with a clean neck puncture that will not clot and a hard nodule forming beneath it. Deena writes "possible deliberate injection" on the form. Sam decides, deliberately, to let it go.

She does not get to. Derek Taylor calls - a first - with six more victims, a smell no animal should have, and an attacker that leaves up fire escapes. They agree to share information "both ways." Hector hints the tox screen found Vysera, which Derek recognizes: the nodule is a normal Vysera injection lump, from a drug so tightly counted that nobody on the trial has ever been short a dose. Tasha maps a corridor up the river wards. Sam chases one attack and arrives a minute late; Derek finds the creature's nest by Graffiti Pier and meets it in daylight - seven feet, cloaked, needle-toothed, with a stinger at the end of a long tongue - and backs away (Interlude DT.4). In Interlude DC.4, it ambushes Rush Order and Dead Drop at a Kingdom-paid construction site and stings Dead Drop, whose power radar reads "three or four powers at once" inside it, including Mr. Nothing's. Sam treats them both.

When Derek returns, the nest has been wire-brushed and bleached. The Auditors connect the tongue to a frog and the frog to the December 2024 zoo theft, and name the Kingdom and Mrs. Xenograft. Amelia sets the book's behavioral rule: the next time Sam's feet start moving, she texts the chat *while* it is happening. On Labor Day Sam hands the case to Councilman Davis, Multiplex, and the DVD lawyer Clara Parker, who teaches her that "belief is not a chain of custody" and gives her an "anytime" number. When the creature stings the fight-club fighter Moloch Horrible, Sam texts in motion, grabs its ankle, and is dragged down a wall. A day later a glowing dockworker is stung and the creature has spines it did not have before. Sam's deduction: the anticoagulant is for taking a blood sample. It is not turning powers off; it is copying them. The city starts calling it "the South Philly Stinger."

Over Rosh Hashanah in Ventnor, Abigail Silverman announces she is moving to Philadelphia to report on "your monster," and the team confirms the copying theory when the creature is seen glowing gold. Faith Song, a very small Boston mutual-aid organizer with a holster under her jacket (Faith Chun, the Red Shocks' Colonel), starts a South Street supply run that Sam and Alex join. The Stinger sheds the glow as a pulsing lump that someone collects before sunrise, keeps Moloch's coral, and stings a man Abigail tracks down: "D.," an unregistered HVAC installer who can make cold. Sam reads it as a radiator and a water supply - the creature no longer needs the river - and names the book's political problem: the Stinger hunts best the people no list has ever reached, and there is no safe way to warn them. Abigail's Inquirer piece puts that line in print. Derek finds the Kingdom's retrieval truck.

Frost events lead to Frankford Creek. Sundial reads a frozen hose - "He's not a monster... He's an animal" - and commands a joint Auditors-Titans recon. At the creek a Kingdom crew is already bagging a whistling mass shed by a fifth, unknown victim: Mudslide, Mr. Preclude (still "Polygraph" to Sam), and a small, talkative woman in a lab coat. The creature takes the bag, frosts Mudslide's arm, and leaves; the woman shouts its name, "Dufay." Preclude recognizes Megalodon and freezes Sam with a spoken command. Interlude LTN.3 then shows the other side: Mrs. Xenograft's beefrogs, the source of Hypeman and Vysera; Mrs. Zygote (Dr. Effie Dunham) presenting **Project Bullfrog** to the Kingdom's Manhattan leadership - Dufay as a 280-pound artificial organism built from Mr. Nothing and Deathgirl cell lines, beefrog tissue, and donor engrams - and Mr. Antithesis's ruling: "We'll let it run." In Interlude LC.1, Lily breaks the freeze with her slingshot, Moonshot pulls Sam out, Zygote covers the retreat with corrosive ten-minute-old constructs, Derek is shot through his vest, and Dufay stings Lily and leaves at her doubled speed.

The morning after, the teams work out the command power (it needs ears in the same air), watch Moonshot's footage of Dufay learning Lily's speed and eating from a bag of garden-center bee pollen, and hear Sundial's read of the handlers: "It's a bag, Z." Dr. Demonic removes Lily's inert nodule and offers to waive his fee if he can keep it; Maggie names the pattern - "he's not stealing powers, he's pirating them." Research turns up Dr. Euphemia Dunham, a Harvard stem-cell prodigy who went private in 2014. The Auditors hand a full file to the DVD. Clara calls it the best she has seen in six years, then declines to log it before Monday's congressional hearing, where it could be subpoenaed. Rampart is sent out of Philadelphia before Dufay can copy him.

That night a costumed figure with a megaphone stages a no-injury fireworks rally on a Center City rooftop - "GIVE US TARGETS. OR GET OUT OF THE WAY" - while Dufay crosses the Ben Franklin Bridge into New Jersey. In Interlude CM.2 the **Boston Red Shocks** follow a tip to a Kingdom warehouse in Grays Ferry: glowing powder shipped on a "Bullfrog" schedule, amphorae marked for return to "Z." The Colonel, 4'10" and drawing weapons from a satchel, stares down Mr. Tyrannosaur with an anti-materiel rifle, and he warns them they are now "on a list." Their public statement names Rex Rampage as a Kingdom enforcer.

On Monday, September 28, Sam watches the hearing on C-SPAN in AP Chemistry. Asked whether a creature is hunting powered residents, Davis answers "Yes" and uses the moment to tell secretly powered kids to tell somebody. The same morning the Boston Globe runs Abigail's article (Interlude AS.1): Vysera sold to the NSRA by a shell company, foreign human DNA in an excised nodule. In a school library Sam concludes that Vysera is grown from Mr. Nothing's cells and that she has been hunting "something that's being milked." Davis refuses under oath to name his source, Abigail goes into hiding, and Clara's first question on the phone is whether Sam is safe. Part 1 ends when Zara calls: her father has gone to a gathering at City Hall. Sam texts the chat while it is happening and heads out with Maggie to watch.

## Themes

- **The wire, not the fist.** Sam tries to be the person who texts, documents, and hands evidence up. The book tests whether that works when institutions cannot act without paper ("belief is not a chain of custody") and when Clara has to protect her by not using what she gave.
- **There is no list.** The Stinger hunts people who have kept their powers secret, and the only way to warn them risks exposing them to a city that has bought sixty percent more guns. Davis's televised "Yes" is the closest thing to a warning anyone is allowed to give.
- **An animal, not a monster.** Derek, Sundial, and Lily each meet Dufay and come away seeing an animal that grooms itself, fails at tricks, and wants to be left alone - while Mrs. Xenograft asks "how many people is Dufay?"
- **Pirating.** Nobody who gets stung stays hurt, so nobody has anything to show a cop. The harm is in the copy the Kingdom keeps.
- **Outpaced by the loud.** The Auditors spend two years being careful; the Red Shocks out Rex Rampage in a weekend, and Abigail prints what Clara said to sit on.
- **Presence.** Sam's dissociation - the "UFO" and "fisheye" zoom-outs, and the ear-pop "Lights, camera" return - is named on-page. The fights stop landing; manual labor and hunger bring her back.
